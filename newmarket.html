The real problem

  1. Buyers search for cert + process + material. Sites say “precision quality since 1987.”
  2. Work still comes from referrals and shows. The site does not win RFQs.
  3. AS9100, ITAR, and first-article work are the ticket. Marketing cannot fake that.
  4. Owners think marketing means ads. They need a shop that a procurement engineer will trust in 30 seconds.
  5. They want Anduril / SpaceX / Rocket Lab work as legacy Boeing work moves. They do not know how to look like that supply chain.

What they hack together now

  • A nephew-built website and a PDF capabilities sheet
  • Thomasnet / GlobalSpec listings they forget to update
  • Booth at a show, then no follow-up
  • Owner on LinkedIn with a 2014 headshot
  • Hoping a current Boeing buyer introduces them

How Brandastic can support this

  • Site + messaging. Make the shop look like a 2026 supplier, not a 1998 machine shop.
  • Get found. Capability + cert + material pages. Google and AI search. Same SEO + AEO motion as the audit hopper, pointed at shops.
  • Show kit. One-pager, follow-up, badge-scan to meeting. Not “we will intro you to Anduril.”
  • Where we sit. Costa Mesa. Anduril is here. Plants are Garden Grove, Anaheim, Huntington, Torrance, El Segundo, Long Beach, San Diego.

What we will not sell

  • Getting on a SpaceX or Anduril approved vendor list
  • ITAR / quality / contracting advice
  • Google Ads as the first product
  • A new Brandastic brand that dumps DESS and the current book
  • Cold “we know people at the primes”

Pricing and money

  • Most shops will not start at $10k/mo ads. That is the current Brandastic close filter, not this wedge.
  • Open at $5–8k/mo for site, search, and follow-up. Diagnostic first.
  • Paid media only after the site can convert an RFQ.
  • Year-one math: 3 retainers at $6k = $18k/mo. That is a practice line, not a new company.
  • LTV can be long if we become their outside marketing person. CAC has to be Justin’s time plus shows, not paid social.

Why this can last a decade

  • Defense restock, Space Force, commercial space, reshoring.
  • SoCal cluster is not a trend. It is infrastructure.
  • Supplier marketing stays bad because owners run plants, not brands.
  • Vulnerable if we only sell “AI search” or if defense budgets reverse. The site + trust job still exists.
Why this fails: we promise access to primes. Owners smell it. Echo-Factory already has proof. Sales cycles are 6–18 months. Shops will not pay $8k for a pretty site if RFQs do not move. Brandastic has zero aerospace logos today.
Why it can work: we are already in Costa Mesa. We already know how to rebuild a site and get a company found. The first three SoCal logos beat any vertical brand. Walk the October show before spending on a booth.

Smallest test that still proves demand

  1. Hand-build 40 SoCal shops. Owner name, site, certs, size.
  2. Justin takes 10 conversations. No deck. Ask where work comes from and what the site is for.
  3. Walk AFCEA Space Industry Days, Oct 21–22, LAX Marriott, as a visitor. 20 supplier conversations. No booth.
  4. If 3+ owners will pay for a scorecard or a site, keep going. If they only want intros, kill it.

Assumptions that can be wrong

  • They have $5k/mo. Many do not.
  • The owner cares about the website. Some only care about the next PO.
  • Costa Mesa is a door-opener. It might just be geography.
  • SEO + AEO is the hook. For this buyer, “look like a real supplier” may beat “AI search.”
  • We can learn enough language (RFQ, FAI, NADCAP) in 30 days to not sound like a consumer shop.
