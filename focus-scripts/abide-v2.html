Abide — Script V2

John 15:5 · ~6–8 min · Film-ready teleprompter

TELEPROMPTER / READ VERSION

Hey — I’m Justin.

Can I ask you something real quick?

How many of you feel like you’re trying really hard to be a “good Christian”…
and you’re still tired?

Like you’re grunting your way through prayer.
Forcing the quiet time.
Checking the boxes.
And deep down you’re wondering why it still feels dry.

I’ve been there.

I’ve been the guy who thought if I just tried harder — more discipline, more mornings, more willpower — then the fruit would show up.

And some weeks it looked okay on the outside.
But on the inside I was a branch trying to squeeze out grapes with my bare hands.

That’s why John 15 hit me so hard.

It’s the night before the cross.

Jesus is with His closest friends.
He’s not giving them a 40-step productivity plan.
He’s not handing them a hustle sermon.

He keeps saying one word.
Over and over.

In English we say abide.
Or remain.

And once you see what that word actually means…
it takes the pressure off your whole walk with God.

Here’s the verse. John 15:5.

Jesus says:

“I am the vine; you are the branches.
Whoever abides in me and I in him, he it is that bears much fruit,
for apart from me you can do nothing.”

Read that slow.

I am the vine. You are the branches.

Picture a vineyard.
A branch doesn’t wake up stressed.
It doesn’t hold a meeting about grape production.
It doesn’t grit its teeth and try to “manifest fruit.”

It stays attached.
And the life of the vine does the rest.

Now here’s the treasure.

That word abide — in the Greek it’s menō.

Jesus uses it something like ten times in a few verses.
It means remain. Stay. Dwell.
Make your home somewhere.

Not visit.
Live there.

So Jesus isn’t saying, “Try harder this week.”
He’s saying, “Stay.”

Make your home in Me.
Don’t just drop by on Sunday.
Don’t treat Me like a drive-through when life gets loud.
Live here.

And then He drops the line most of us skip:

“Apart from me you can do nothing.”

Not “not much.”
Nothing.

A branch snapped off the vine can still look green for a day.
It can still look impressive.
But it’s already dying.
It has no life of its own.

That was me in seasons of striving.
Busy. Productive. Looking fine.
And slowly disconnected.

Here’s the freeing part.

If fruit comes from staying connected — not from striving —
then the pressure comes off.

You were never meant to manufacture a godly life by sheer willpower.
You were meant to stay close to Jesus and let His life flow through you.

The fruit is His work.
Your job is to abide.

That’s who Jesus is.
He doesn’t stand over you with a clipboard demanding more grapes.
He invites you to come home and stay.

He’s the source.
He’s the life.
He’s the vine.

Everything good grows out of one thing:
staying near Him.

That’s why opening the Bible matters.
Not to check a box.
Just to stay close to Him.

So here’s what I’m doing this week — and you can do it too.

Every day, I’m going to take a few quiet minutes with Jesus.

Maybe with my coffee and my Bible.
Maybe on a walk, talking to Him.
Maybe five minutes before I go to bed.

Not because I’m trying to earn anything.
Not to look spiritual.
Just to be with Him.

That’s it.

Don’t try harder. Just stay close.

Abide.
Stay with Jesus.
He’ll take care of the fruit.

That’s the word.
Abide.

Not to strive — but to stay.
The whole Christian life in one word.

I’m rooting for you.
