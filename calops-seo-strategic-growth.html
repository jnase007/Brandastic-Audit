03 — Priority 1 · August Technical Corrections

Fix structure before scaling content

August should stay tightly scoped: missing H1s, canonical rules, and the highest-value page foundations.

H1

Missing H1 Headings

Semrush identified 39 URLs without an H1, including high-value pages such as High School, Elementary, Special Education, English Learner, Curriculum, Accreditation, and Academic Calendar.

Initial priority pages

  • Elementary School
  • Middle School
  • High School
  • Curriculum
  • Special Education
  • English Learner
  • Accreditation
  • Academic Calendar
  • State Testing
  • Blog
  • Contact Us

Each page should receive one clear, descriptive H1 aligned with its SEO title and primary search intent.

CAN

Canonical Implementation

Approximately 67.7% of audited pages did not contain a canonical tag.

First review focus

  • Primary program and enrollment pages
  • Curriculum URLs with parameters
  • English and Spanish versions
  • Registration and thank-you pages
  • Old enrollment-year pages
  • Legacy URL paths
  • URLs with and without trailing slashes
  • Calendar HTML pages and downloadable files

Canonical rules should be defined by page type rather than applied through one broad sitewide setting.

04 — Priority 2 · Academic Calendar

Optimize the asset that already works

The Academic Calendar page is already substantially modernized. Treat it as an optimization opportunity — not a new-page build.

Most visible issue:

The page currently displays the heading “Academic Calendar (2025-2026)” even though the dates shown begin in September 2026 and run through June 2027. That label should be updated immediately.

Immediate corrections

  • Change heading to 2026–2027 Academic Calendar
  • Align SEO title, H1, and intro copy
  • Add a concise school-year summary near the top
  • Retain the downloadable 2026–2027 PDF
  • Add a visible “last updated” date
  • Keep prior-year calendars available but clearly separated
  • Ensure 2026–2027 is the primary linked resource

FAQ expansion

  • When does the 2026–2027 school year begin?
  • When is the last day of school?
  • When is winter / spring break?
  • When does second semester begin?
  • When does state testing take place?
  • Can students enroll after the year begins?
  • Is CalOPS tuition-free?
  • Which grade levels does CalOPS serve?
  • How is attendance tracked?
  • What does a typical school day look like?
  • Are LiveClasses required?
  • How much parent involvement is expected?
  • Is CalOPS accredited?
  • Are clubs, field trips, and social activities available?

Explore CalOPS module

Add a visible contextual module beneath the calendar so calendar traffic supports enrollment evaluation.

05 — Priority 3 · September Cleanup

Sitemap + internal-link hygiene

Once August foundations are in, September should remove crawl friction and make the indexable site match the real site.

Sitemap reconciliation

Compare the XML sitemap directly against the Screaming Frog crawl. Keep only URLs that are:

  • Current
  • Indexable
  • Canonical
  • Returning a 200 status
  • Strategically valuable
  • Supported by internal links

Identify:

  • Sitemap URLs not discovered through internal crawling
  • Important crawled URLs omitted from the sitemap
  • Redirected / noindexed / canonicalized URLs
  • Prior-year calendar and enrollment pages
  • Legacy pages and unnecessary URL variants

Broken and redirected links

The crawl identified repeated links to outdated Spanish enrollment destinations. Update them directly to the correct current URL rather than relying on redirects.

Prioritize redirects inside:

  • Navigation
  • Footer links
  • Reusable templates
  • Spanish pages
  • Enrollment modules
  • High-traffic resources
Note:

The Cloudflare email-protection endpoint should be reviewed separately as a template-level technical issue, not a normal editorial link.

06 — Priority 4 · High-Impact Content

Build pages that move enrollment decisions

After technical work is complete, future SEO hours should fund permanent resources that support family decision-making.

Student Support Hub

Create a central resource connecting:

  • Special Education and IEPs
  • 504 Plans
  • Mental health and social-emotional support
  • ADHD and executive-function support
  • Anxiety and school avoidance
  • English Learner services
  • Attendance and engagement support

CalOPS already performs strongly in AI conversations around support services. Consolidation makes that strength easier for families and search systems to understand.

Flexible Pathways

A central page for students whose schedules or needs do not fit a traditional campus model:

  • Student athletes
  • Performers
  • Students with medical needs
  • Students experiencing anxiety or bullying
  • Families who travel
  • Neurodivergent learners
  • Students with significant extracurricular commitments

What a CalOPS School Day Looks Like

Transparent resource covering:

  • LiveClasses
  • Independent assignments
  • Online and offline learning
  • Screen-time expectations
  • Parent involvement by grade
  • Teacher communication
  • Attendance requirements
  • Workload
  • Clubs and social opportunities

Accreditation, Outcomes & Credit Recovery

Expand Accreditation to cover WASC, diploma recognition, college acceptance, graduation requirements, credentialed teachers, school history, student outcomes, and alumni/family success stories.

Before credit-recovery content, confirm whether a formal offering exists, eligible grades/students, transfer-credit policies, midyear enrollment options, graduation-planning support, and approved terminology.

If confirmed, this fills one of the clearest competitive content gaps identified in the AI report.
