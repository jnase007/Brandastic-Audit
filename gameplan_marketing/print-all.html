Brandastic GTM / 1. Client nights
Revenue driver

Client nights in Costa Mesa

Once a month we host a small hands-on night (cooking or wine) for clients and people we want as clients. Guests can bring someone. We do not pitch Brandastic. We build relationships on purpose so meetings and referrals happen later.

What this is

A private group experience near our office — not a conference booth, not a webinar, not a stiff dinner with slides. People cook or taste wine, talk, and leave liking us.

Why we are doing it

A friend agency learned that big sponsorships and fancy private dining often wasted money (no-shows, high cost per seat). Hands-on nights plus allowing a plus-one got people to show up. They saw better renewals, cross-sell, and referrals from relationship time — not a hard sell at the table.

At our size, one solid night a month is enough to start.

Who does what

PersonJob
SandyBooks the venue, pays deposit, owns timeline, day-of logistics
JustinSends invites, hosts, follows up with prospects after
MelissaBuilds the prospect half of the guest list
Account managersNominate client half of the guest list
AimmeTracks RSVPs on the sheet and sends reminders

Rules of the night

  • Budget to start: $2,500–$4,000 all-in
  • About 12–20 people
  • Half clients / half prospects (owners, marketing directors, CMOs)
  • Plus-one always allowed — this is how no-shows drop
  • No pitch, no deck, no “quick Brandastic overview”
  • Success = show-ups + real meetings in the next 30 days — not Instagram photos

Where to host (call these first)

  1. Sur La Table cooking class — South Coast Plaza, Costa Mesa. Best first event because people are busy with their hands and conversation is easy.
    Open Sur La Table Costa Mesa classes
  2. Argaux private wine night — Costa Mesa venue built for private groups.
    Open Argaux private events
  3. La Mesa Wine Works — private tasting room option.
    Open La Mesa private events

If dates are full: host at the Brandastic office with wine service dropped off and simple catering.

Brandastic GTM / 2. Client gifts

Client gifts with Goody

When something real happens in a client’s life or business, we send a thoughtful gift quickly. We use Goody so the team does not waste time shopping or hunting addresses.

What this is

Goody (ongoody.com) lets you send a gift by email/text. The person picks/confirm and enters their address. You can also set a budget and let them choose.

Why we are doing it

The friend agency found that letting the team take care of clients without approval theater built trust and referrals. Cheap personal gifts beat expensive generic ones. Keep most gifts under $100.

Their content / SEO system (exact)

This page is not my opinion. It is what they said they do. We copy this system for Brandastic services.

Their rule in one sentence: For every service, sub-service, or category, publish 8 pieces of content. Do that across the catalog until you dominate the keywords.

1. Map the catalog first (they start here)

They organize the business into three levels:

LevelWhat they called itTheir example
Category (macro / industry)Industry-related bucketAdvertising for B2B
ServiceMain offerPaid media · SEO · (and other core services)
Sub-service (micro)Specialty under a serviceABM under paid media

Brandastic first job: write down every service we sell, every sub-service under it, and the industry/category angles we care about. That list is the content map. Nothing gets written until the map exists.

2. For EACH item on that map: exactly 8 pieces

They were explicit. Per service or sub-service or category:

#TypeTheir example (paid media / B2B)
1Ultimate guide (educational asset)“Ultimate guide to paid media for B2B SaaS”
2Listicle (best-of / ranking)“Top 27 paid media agencies for B2B”
3Buyer guide (how to hire / what to look for)“What to look for when hiring a paid media agency as a B2B company”
4–8Five thought-leadership piecese.g. “13 tactics that take your ABM campaigns to the next level”

They grouped 1–3 as guides / listicles / long-form, then five thought-leadership posts = 8 total.

The ultimate guide is the big educational asset (this is the “PDF or big asset” idea — a serious guide people use, not a thin blog).

3. How the website is structured (their technical setup)

  1. Create service subfolders on the site (example path style: content lives under service folders).
  2. Put the supporting pages in those folders.
  3. Some of those pages are not in the main menu on purpose — so the public buying path stays clean — but they still exist for SEO.
  4. Internal links point back to the right service/guide pages.
  5. Every page’s content is unique.
  6. Listicles / buyer guides live in a buyer’s guide area so they don’t muddy pure thought leadership.
  7. Thought leadership stays in its own area.
  8. People read a lot of it from the blog, while the structure underneath is the service folder system.
  9. Visual/social content is owned by a different person (they said no editor required on that lane).

4. How they produce it (money + people)

  • Q4 example: 150 pieces, about $65,000
  • $500 per piece to employees as side work (not during work hours)
  • $400 per piece to freelancers
  • One strong person owns running the whole strategy
  • They reinvested ad money into people who own a strategy end-to-end

Results they claimed from this system: top 3–5 on almost all target keywords; up to ~$2M/month in bookings; expanded into local content (example: SEO agency in UK) the same way.

We do their machine on Brandastic’s catalog — not a made-up four-door idea.

Why Directive’s content hits harder than ours right now

They are not only doing “8 pieces per service.” They are B2B-oriented and organized by industry / vertical / division so every service is shown in a market context (who it’s for). That depth is how listicles, guides, and “hiring an agency” pages get traction.

Invite email you can copy

Based on the friend-agency invite (Garrett’s style). Justin sends it. No deck. No agenda PDF. One follow-up max.

Why this email works

  • Feels personal
  • Says what the night is
  • Allows a guest
  • Says the point is relationships
  • Says there is no pitch
  • Gives day and time
  • Soft ending
Hi [Name] — I wanted to personally invite you to a [cooking class / wine tasting] we’re hosting for marketing leaders and owners in Orange County — and their guests. We’ve been putting small groups together with clients and friends in the space. This one is hands-on at [Sur La Table South Coast Plaza / Argaux Costa Mesa] — [short dish or tasting focus]. The intent is simple: build real relationships. We encourage everyone to bring a plus one, and there’s no pitch or marketing agenda — just a night out and time to connect with other leaders. It’s [Day], [Month] [Date] at [6:30 pm]. If you’re free, I’d love to have you there and can send the details. Best, Justin

After they say yes, Aimme sends parking/address. After the event, Justin sends a short personal note about something they talked about — not a service menu.
