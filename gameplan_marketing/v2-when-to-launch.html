Build vs buy + org setup — full detail

Why they killed M&A and how they structured the company to launch divisions. Use this for org + make-vs-buy decisions.

1. Why buying agencies failed

  • Tried to acquire a communications firm and an ecommerce firm under the old model.
  • Founders they could afford were not better than leaders already inside (named bar: Larissa, Lauren).
  • Targets had revenue but weak contracts, weak differentiation, weak strategic depth.
  • Paying a multiple on revenue did not buy enough other assets (team quality, IP, process).
  • Work-ethic / expectation mismatch with sellers (part of why they were sellable).
  • Just running deal origination + management cost about $45,000/month.
  • Hard to find clean targets at all.
  • Message to sub-$10M agencies: treat acquisition as a warning — often worse than building.

2. What they did instead

  • Folded M&A energy into a platform / co-strategy posture.
  • Went back to incubation of divisions internally.
  • Prior proof: incubated a P&L to about $3M in under 12 months — more efficient, more control, more enjoyable.
  • Wanted communications + ecommerce capabilities; could not find good B2B versions to buy cleanly; built them.
  • Already ranking #1 in LLMs for “best B2B ecommerce agency / best B2B PR firm” before formally offering those services — used as signal to launch for real (late Feb/March; website + kit live quickly).

3. Org architecture they built

B2B industry macros

Every B2B industry rolls into three buckets:

  1. Technology
  2. Industrial
  3. Services

Divisions

  • Performance — core, about $30M P&L
  • Commerce — new
  • Communications — new; reverse-integrate earned media into performance as earned + owned POV

CS / Account Director layer

  • Trigger: client spend > $20K AND multiple services
  • Gets an Account Director
  • Why: SEO + media + creative strategists each running separate weekly client meetings → inefficiency + churn
  • Centralize multi-service over $20K under one AD
  • AD pay: base about $151K–$175K, OTE under $200K
  • Moved strong strategists into AD roles at same salary to avoid turnover before they were “director ready,” while giving a path
  • Big deals ($20K+ or $50K+): pair AD with AE on the pitch because buyers ask “who’s on my account?”

Launch operating rhythm

  • Monday + Thursday, 1 hour
  • Required: head of comms, commerce, sales, marketing, CS, finance, HR
  • CEO gives roadmap templates blank; leaders fill dates/notes
  • CEO designs structure; leaders own the how

4. Brandastic tickets from this section

  1. Default decision: build, don’t buy unless operator clearly beats our leads and book is clean.
  2. Define multi-service $ threshold for a single account owner (their pattern: >$20K).
  3. If launching anything new: Mon/Thu roadmap meeting with all functions, blank status template from Justin.
  4. Map our industries into a small set of macros/verticals (their three: tech/industrial/services) before content/ads sprawl.
