Education · the full picture

TAM · targets · channels · recommendation

QuestionAnswer
How big?44,000+ schools in the US. Charter (~7,500), private K-12 (~29,730), colleges (~5,999), online/virtual (~700+). Total education marketing spend: $4.7B+/year. Higher ed digital alone: $2.77B. SoCal: ~3,000–4,000 schools. Sources: NCES, IPEDS, EAB, Simpson Scarborough.
What we'd focus onOnline and hybrid charter schools that depend on search for enrollment. Private K-12 with real tuition revenue ($50K–$500K marketing budgets). Small-to-mid colleges that can't afford a big agency. Start with CalOPS peers (online-heavy public schools).
Revenue at 10%~700–1,400 addressable schools × $7K avg package × 12 = $5.9M–$11.8M/year. Even 1% = $3.4M+ (more than the 2030 goal from one segment alone).
Apollo targetingTitles: Director of Enrollment, VP Marketing, Director of Marketing, Head of School, Superintendent, Chief Growth Officer, Dean of Admissions.
Filters: Industry = Education. Sub-type = charter, private, online. Size = 50–2,000 employees. Location = start SoCal, expand national for online schools.
Where to find themLinkedIn outbound (enrollment/marketing leaders). Direct email with CalOPS proof. SEO targeting "enrollment marketing," "school digital marketing," "charter school marketing." Education conferences (NACAC, charter school summits, CASE). Referral from CalOPS to peer schools.
Proof we haveCalOPS at $21,000/month. Live case. The enrollment pain is real and every school has it.
Recommendation: This is the best growth bet. Biggest market, best proof, highest revenue potential, national reach. If the team picks one market, this is the one.

Dental / implant manufacturing · the full picture

TAM · targets · channels · recommendation

QuestionAnswer
How big?Abutment/implant manufacturers: only ~35 in the US ($1.1B market). Dental labs: 4,216–9,341. Supply distributors: 473+. Equipment manufacturers: 550+. The niche we know (abutments) is tiny. The broader dental supply chain is much bigger. Sources: iData Research, IBISWorld, KokoQuest.
What we'd focus onToday: companies like Preat, Endes USA — dental abutment/implant component manufacturers. To scale: expand into dental labs and supply distributors that need SEO to reach dentists. NOT general dentist offices.
Revenue at 10%Abutments only: 1–2 clients = $48K–$168K (too small). Expanded to labs + distributors: 96–170 clients = $4.6M–$8.2M. The gap between these two numbers is the whole question.
Apollo targetingTitles: Owner, President, GM, VP Sales, Marketing Manager.
Filters: Industry = Medical Devices, Dental. Sub-type = manufacturer, supplier, lab. Size = 10–200 employees. NAICS = 339114 (dental equipment), 339116 (dental labs). Keywords = implant, abutment, prosthetic, crown, bridge.
Where to find themTrade shows (Chicago Dental Society Midwinter, Greater New York Dental Meeting, ADA). LinkedIn (owner/GM titles at dental manufacturers). Direct email with case studies from Salvin, Young Surgical, Dental Smart. Industry directories (KokoQuest, Thomas). SEO is slower here — relationship-driven market.
Proof we haveSalvin ($4.9K/mo), Paragon ($3.5K/mo), Young Surgical ($4.9K/mo), Dental Smart ($7.8K/mo). Real clients, real results.
Recommendation: Keep current dental clients happy and growing. But do NOT build the growth strategy around abutment manufacturers — there are only 35 of them. If we want dental to scale, we need to test expanding into dental labs and distributors. Explore, don't bet the farm.
BRANDASTIC 2027DENTAL
Aero / defense manufacturing · the full picture

TAM · targets · channels · recommendation

QuestionAnswer
How big?SoCal has hundreds of small aero/defense Tier 2/3 suppliers. No public count of all small shops. OC alone: 26+ companies surveyed (~21K employees). Marketing budgets are small: $20K–$80K/year. Sources: OC Business Journal, industry estimates.
What we'd focus onOwner-led SoCal shops with 10–200 employees. AS9100 / ITAR certified. Precision machining, fabrication, surface treatment. NOT primes like Boeing or Raytheon.
Revenue at 10%~175–330 hirable companies × $4K avg × 12 = $864K–$1.6M/year. Decent but smaller packages and slower sales cycles.
Apollo targetingTitles: Owner, President, VP Operations, Business Development Manager.
Filters: Industry = Aerospace & Defense, Manufacturing. Size = 10–200 employees. Location = LA, OC, San Diego, Riverside, San Bernardino counties. Keywords = AS9100, ITAR, CNC, precision, machining, fabrication.
Where to find themIn person — this is a relationship market. SoCal manufacturing events, NTMA meetings, local chambers. LinkedIn (owner titles at SoCal shops). Referral from Morehouse, Wearparts, LabTech. Direct email is harder — owners are skeptical of marketing.
Proof we haveMorehouse ($6.3K/mo), Wearparts ($3.9K/mo), LabTech ($6.1K/mo). We know this buyer.
Recommendation: Keep current clients happy. Take referrals. Do NOT pour outbound campaigns into this segment — the budgets are too small and the sales cycle is too long for it to be the growth engine.
