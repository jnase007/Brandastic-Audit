Brandastic TAM / ICP — how we build the list like they did

They did not buy a random list and run AirPods ads. Their paid machine sits on a year-curated account list tied to a tight ideal market. Until Brandastic locks that, campaigns are guesswork.

What they actually did (scale): started around ~20,000 accounts → curated down toward ~10,000 ideal targets → that list took on the order of a year to get right → then Primer upload, offers (AirPods / Kindle / $105 Amazon), Meta webinars, LinkedIn message ads. Peak proof: 10K accounts → 6,800 calls → $6M→$22M.

What we must not skip: Brandastic’s target market definition first. No list source, no campaign, until triple-niche is written.

1. Honest status: Brandastic does not have a locked TAM yet

Public site still reads full-service OC agency (SEO, PPC, Shopify, web, social, branding) for “businesses nationwide.” That is boil-the-ocean positioning — exactly what they said kills paid.

Book of business (sample active roster) is mixed. That is a feature for reverse-engineering ICP — not a finished TAM.

  2. Multi-location / ecommerce consumer brands that buy social+Shopify+creative, OR
  3. A deliberate mix — but then we need two separate TAMs and two campaign systems, not one mushy list.

4. How we pull the list (once niche is locked)

Same spine as them — sized for Brandastic (not 10K on day one).

Step A — Seed sources (pick tools by niche)

  • Lookalike from best clients — export top 10 by margin/retention; list 50–100 peers each (competitors, associations, trade show exhibitors).
  • Industry directories — dental/med device: association member lists, dealer networks; industrial: Thomasnet-style directories; ecommerce: Shopify app ecosystem / DTC databases.
  • Data tools — Apollo, ZoomInfo, Clay, LinkedIn Sales Nav, BuiltWith (tech install), Similarweb/SEMrush for traffic peers.
  • Primer (or equivalent) later for ad platform match — after verify, not before.
  • Not a purchased “OC businesses” email dump.

Step B — Start wide, narrow hard (their scale → our scale)

Step D — 100 random scrolls before any Brandastic ad dollar

Same as them: if you would not bet owner distribution on this list, do not run AirPods/$105 offers yet.

5. How campaigns attach once TAM exists

Only after Steps 3–4:

  1. Offer from their ranked stack sized to ACV: AirPods / Kindle / ~$105 Amazon — gift after show-up/call.
  2. Message to VP/owner change agent — division/outcome language; problem specific to the niche (like their product-feed/margins line for commerce).
  3. Channel by audience — Meta webinars if buyers live there; LinkedIn message ads if B2B professional; don’t force one channel for every niche.
  4. Webinar — AEs pitch signups; experts present; AE owns follow-up (Q4 Panic pattern).
  5. Content/SEO 8-pack only for the same niche keywords (V01+V03) so organic and paid reinforce one story.
  6. Measure influence pipeline + held meetings including gift COGS — not vanity CPL.

If we run two niches, we run two TAMs, two offers, two creative lanes — not one campaign hoping both convert.

6. Workshop agenda for Justin (90 minutes)

  1. Rank current clients by profit + ease + retention (not just revenue).
  2. Circle the top cluster — that is the default paid niche candidate.
  3. Write triple niche sentence + exclusions.
  4. Pick list sources for that niche only.
  5. Assign owner for 90-day v1 list (500–1,500 verified).
  6. Park ads until 100-scroll QA passes.

Output: one Google Sheet — ICP tab + TAM v1 tab + campaign brief stub.

Bottom line: Their 20K→10K year-long list is the product of a sharp ICP. Brandastic’s throwaway risk is running $105 gift campaigns without deciding if we are a dental/med-device B2B shop, an industrial product shop, a multi-location retail shop — or intentionally two systems. Lock the niche, then pull the list, then build campaigns. Not the reverse.
