Their content / SEO system (exact)

This page is not my opinion. It is what they said they do. We copy this system for Brandastic services.

Their rule in one sentence: For every service, sub-service, or category, publish 8 pieces of content. Do that across the catalog until you dominate the keywords.

1. Map the catalog first (they start here)

They organize the business into three levels:

LevelWhat they called itTheir example
Category (macro / industry)Industry-related bucketAdvertising for B2B
ServiceMain offerPaid media · SEO · (and other core services)
Sub-service (micro)Specialty under a serviceABM under paid media

Brandastic first job: write down every service we sell, every sub-service under it, and the industry/category angles we care about. That list is the content map. Nothing gets written until the map exists.

2. For EACH item on that map: exactly 8 pieces

They were explicit. Per service or sub-service or category:

#TypeTheir example (paid media / B2B)
1Ultimate guide (educational asset)“Ultimate guide to paid media for B2B SaaS”
2Listicle (best-of / ranking)“Top 27 paid media agencies for B2B”
3Buyer guide (how to hire / what to look for)“What to look for when hiring a paid media agency as a B2B company”
4–8Five thought-leadership piecese.g. “13 tactics that take your ABM campaigns to the next level”

They grouped 1–3 as guides / listicles / long-form, then five thought-leadership posts = 8 total.

The ultimate guide is the big educational asset (this is the “PDF or big asset” idea — a serious guide people use, not a thin blog).

3. How the website is structured (their technical setup)

  1. Create service subfolders on the site (example path style: content lives under service folders).
  2. Put the supporting pages in those folders.
  3. Some of those pages are not in the main menu on purpose — so the public buying path stays clean — but they still exist for SEO.
  4. Internal links point back to the right service/guide pages.
  5. Every page’s content is unique.
  6. Listicles / buyer guides live in a buyer’s guide area so they don’t muddy pure thought leadership.
  7. Thought leadership stays in its own area.
  8. People read a lot of it from the blog, while the structure underneath is the service folder system.
  9. Visual/social content is owned by a different person (they said no editor required on that lane).

4. How they produce it (money + people)

  • Q4 example: 150 pieces, about $65,000
  • $500 per piece to employees as side work (not during work hours)
  • $400 per piece to freelancers
  • One strong person owns running the whole strategy
  • They reinvested ad money into people who own a strategy end-to-end

Results they claimed from this system: top 3–5 on almost all target keywords; up to ~$2M/month in bookings; expanded into local content (example: SEO agency in UK) the same way.

5. Brandastic application (same system, our services)

We do their machine on Brandastic’s catalog — not a made-up four-door idea.

Step A — Build the map (week 1)

Write every:

  • Service (examples to confirm with Justin): Paid media/PPC, SEO, Websites, Creative, Email/CRM, Strategy / fractional leadership, etc.
  • Sub-services under each (examples): Google Ads, Meta Ads, Local SEO, Ecommerce SEO, Shopify builds, etc.
  • Categories / industries we sell into (examples): home services, dental, ecommerce, professional services, etc.)

That spreadsheet is the master content plan. Each row = one “node” that needs 8 pieces.

Step B — For each node, assign the 8 titles

  1. Ultimate guide (long educational asset — page + optional PDF download)
  2. Top list / best-of listicle
  3. What to look for when hiring [service] (buyer guide)
  4. Thought leadership #1
  5. Thought leadership #2
  6. Thought leadership #3
  7. Thought leadership #4
  8. Thought leadership #5

Step C — Site build

  • Create service subfolders on brandastic.com
  • Publish guides/listicles into a buyer’s guide section
  • Keep thought leadership separate
  • Internal link everything back to the service pages
  • Do not force every SEO page into the main nav

Step D — Production

  • One owner runs the system (CJ or content lead)
  • Pay staff side-work or freelancers per asset (they used $500 staff / $400 freelance — we can start and adjust)
  • Not during core client hours for staff side work
  • Ship node by node (finish 8 for Paid Media before boiling the ocean)

Worked example for Brandastic (Paid Media node)

  1. Ultimate Guide to Paid Media for Growing Brands (big asset / PDF optional)
  2. Top Paid Media Agencies for [industry we serve]
  3. What to Look for When Hiring a Paid Media Agency
  4. Thought leadership: hooks that actually convert
  5. Thought leadership: when to kill an ad set
  6. Thought leadership: Google vs Meta for lead gen
  7. Thought leadership: creative testing system
  8. Thought leadership: agency vs in-house paid team

Then repeat the same 8-pattern for SEO, Websites, each sub-service, and each priority industry category.

Before the 8-piece machine

Why Directive’s content hits harder than ours right now

They are not only doing “8 pieces per service.” They are B2B-oriented and organized by industry / vertical / division so every service is shown in a market context (who it’s for). That depth is how listicles, guides, and “hiring an agency” pages get traction.

Brandastic today: service list is wide, but we are scattered. Not clearly locked as B2B. Ideal target market is not fully decided. Content without verticals will stay generic.

What we must lock (Justin + leadership)

  1. Primary orientation: B2B brands / operators (not broad B2C consumer plays), unless we explicitly choose otherwise.
  2. 3–5 ideal verticals we will own (not 20). Draft list to debate:
    • Ecommerce / product brands
    • Home services / local multi-location
    • Health / dental / medical practices
    • Education / online schools
    • B2B professional / industrial (if we want their lane)
  3. Ideal client profile: revenue band, who signs (owner / CMO / marketing lead), min monthly retainer or project size, geo (OC + remote OK?).
  4. Service × vertical grid: only build 8-packs where service meets a chosen vertical (example: SEO for ecommerce, Paid for home services, Photo/Video for product brands).

How the site should feel once locked (like their depth)

/services/seo/ /services/seo/ecommerce/ /services/seo/home-services/ /services/seo/guides/ultimate-guide-seo-for-ecommerce (PDF) /services/seo/lists/top-seo-agencies-for-ecommerce /services/seo/guides/what-to-look-for-hiring-seo-agency-ecommerce /blog/... thought leadership linked back

Main nav stays clean. Depth lives in subfolders + blog. Every piece answers: service + who it’s for.

Order of work: (1) Lock orientation + 3–5 verticals + ICP · (2) Map service × vertical nodes · (3) Run their 8-piece pack per node · (4) Tinny/AI drafts, human publishes.
Do not spray 8 pieces across every service with no market. That recreates scatter.
