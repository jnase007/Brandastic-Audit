The real problem

  1. Buyers search cert + process + material; sites say “precision quality since 1987.”
  2. Work still comes from referrals and shows. Site does not win RFQs.
  3. Owners think marketing means ads. They need a shop a buyer trusts in 30 seconds.

What we will not sell

  • Getting on a SpaceX/Anduril AVL
  • ITAR / quality / contracting advice
  • Google Ads as the first product
  • Cold “we know people at the primes”

6. How we write to them

How to talk

Point: The shop already does the work. A new buyer shouldn’t have to hunt through a PDF to see it.

Ask: Want a short read on what a buyer finds on your site today?

Tone: Owner to owner. Respect the floor. Never promise primes or AVLs.

Skip these lines

  • “You look like 2009”
  • “We’ll get you on SpaceX / Anduril”
  • “Job shop” (sounds small and rude)
  • Ads-first pitch before the site can take a quote

Brandastic's lane

  • Tier 2 and Tier 3 suppliers — make components and sub-assemblies
  • Typically 10–200 employees, owner-led
  • AS9100 / ITAR certified shops
  • Precision machining, fabrication, surface treatment
  • Most have terrible websites and no real digital presence
  • Current clients: Morehouse ($6.3K), Wearparts ($3.9K), LabTech ($6.1K)

The challenge

  • Relationship-driven industry — buyers know their suppliers
  • Many owners don't believe in marketing
  • Budgets are small: $20K–$80K/year for marketing
  • Sales cycle is long — certifications matter more than websites
  • No public data on exact count of small SoCal aero suppliers
  • SoCal advantage: we are local and can visit shops
