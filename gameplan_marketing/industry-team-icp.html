How big are these industries — and who do we sell to?

Justin locked primary = dental / surgical / medical B2B and secondary = education (CalOps). This page sizes the opportunity and names the titles we should put on the TAM list and in ads.

How to read the numbers: Industry “size” is market revenue (what buyers spend in the category), not Brandastic revenue. Use it to judge whether a TAM of hundreds–thousands of accounts is realistic. Figures are planning ranges from public industry research (IBISWorld-style dental lab reports, dental equipment/supplies outlooks, medical device market summaries, NCES/CDE education counts). Re-verify exact $ before investor-grade decks.

PRIMARY — Dental / surgical / medical B2B

How big is the “team” (addressable companies)?

SliceUS scale (planning range)What it means for us
Dental laboratories On the order of ~8,000–12,000+ commercial labs historically; US lab market often cited around ~$5B–$7B+ range in industry reports (varies by year/source) Core of your current wins (Prudental-style). Plenty of accounts for a 500–2,000 curated TAM without boiling the ocean.
Dental equipment & supplies US market typically multi-billion (often mid–high single-digit $B to low tens $B depending on what’s included: equipment, consumables, digital) Salvin / DESS / supply-style buyers. Overlaps lab channel + dealer networks.
Surgical / med-device / specialty healthcare product US medical device industry is very large (tens to $100B+ depending on definition). We only want a thin slice: specialty suppliers similar to Young Surgical / DESS — not Johnson & Johnson HQ Filter hard by size and product niche so list stays reachable for $5K–$15K retainers.
Brandastic v1 TAM target 500–1,500 verified accounts (from a 2,000–5,000 start list) Matches Directive discipline at our scale (they went ~20K→10K over a year; we start smaller).

Company size to put on the list

  • Sweet spot: ~$5M–$75M revenue or ~20–300 employees
  • Retainer fit: can pay $4.5K–$15K+/mo for SEO+PPC (your public band starts ~$3.5–$7.5K; push toward higher for paid chase)
  • Exclude for paid new-biz: solo tech shops with no marketing budget; mega-enterprise that only buys holding-company agencies; pure DTC consumer brands

Titles to target (primary list + LinkedIn ads)

Primary (put these on the TAM + ads first):

  1. VP of Marketing / Vice President, Marketing
  2. Director of Marketing / Director, Digital Marketing
  3. Head of Marketing / Marketing Leader
  4. Owner / Founder / President / CEO — when company is smaller and founder still owns growth (very common in labs/suppliers)
  5. CMO — when present (less common under ~$50M; still include)

Secondary (useful, not the hero ad persona):

  • Demand Gen Manager, Growth Marketing Manager, Performance Marketing Manager
  • eCommerce Manager (if they sell via web/dealers online)
  • Sales/Marketing hybrid titles at smaller firms (“Sales & Marketing Manager”)

Do not target as primary agency buyer:

  • Dentists / surgeons / clinicians (end customers of your client)
  • Office managers at clinics
  • SEO specialists inside the company (they feel threatened — same rule as Directive V04)
  • Procurement-only contacts until sales cycle needs them

Geo

  • US national for primary TAM (labs and suppliers are nationwide)
  • Optional boost: states with dense dental manufacturing / lab clusters (CA, FL, TX, NY, PA, Midwest manufacturing belts) — but don’t limit the list to OC

How marketing hits this segment

  1. List sources: NADL / state lab associations, dealer directories, lookalikes of your five dental/med clients, Apollo/Clay/ZoomInfo with industry NAICS filters, trade-show exhibitor lists
  2. LinkedIn Message Ads to titles above — problem angles about dealer demand, quote flow, product visibility (not generic “leads”)
  3. Offers: AirPods → Kindle → $105 Amazon after show-up/call
  4. Webinars: AEs pitch; team presents (product-feed style vertical topics)
  5. Content 8-pack: only dental/med/lab keywords first
  6. Relationship first: white-space + reopen inside current dental/med book

SECONDARY — Education (CalOps lane)

How big is the “team”?

SliceScale (planning)What it means
California public K–12 ~1,000+ local educational agencies / districts (plus thousands of schools). CalOps proves you already serve this world. Finite, researchable TAM — good for a curated list, not infinite.
US public K–12 ~13,000 districts nationally (NCES-order magnitude) Only expand outside CA after CA list is sharp and ACV is proven.
Brandastic education v1 TAM 150–400 CA districts/LEAs + relevant ed vendors if you sell to vendors too Separate sheet from dental. Never merge paid audiences.

Company / org size

  • Districts and county offices with enough budget for digital (not single school with $500/mo)
  • Or education-adjacent vendors serving schools (if that matches how CalOps work actually works — confirm with account team)
  • Retainer reality may differ from dental B2B — map CalOps contract shape before copying $105 LinkedIn offers

Titles to target (education)

Primary:

  1. Chief Communications Officer / Director of Communications
  2. Director of Marketing / Marketing & Communications Director (where districts have them)
  3. Public Information Officer (PIO)
  4. Superintendent / Assistant Superintendent (smaller LEAs — often final yes)
  5. Director of Technology / CTO when digital presence sits under tech (common)

Secondary:

  • Community Engagement leads, Enrollment Marketing (charters/private), Webmaster / Digital Media specialists (influencers, not always budget holders)

Avoid as hero: individual school principals as cold paid targets (slow, low ACV unless strategy says so)

Geo

  • Start: California (CalOps proof + density)
  • Expand to other large states only after CA playbook works

How marketing hits education

  1. Mostly relationship + SEO/content delivery first (procurement-heavy)
  2. List from CDE directories, county offices, conference attendee lists
  3. Paid later only if average deal size clears gift/webinar math
  4. Messaging: enrollment, community trust, clarity for parents — not “B2B pipeline”
