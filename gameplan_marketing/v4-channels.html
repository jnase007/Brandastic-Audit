Meta · LinkedIn · gift cards — full detail

Channel section expanded for media plans and tickets.

Rule: Meta owns webinars. LinkedIn is ABM/content + legacy customer-gen. Gift cards manufacture show-up intent. Non-incentive “free audit” retarget does not work.

1. Meta (primary for webinars)

  • “Meta is for webinars.” Absolutely crushing.
  • Can go upmarket (CMO-level lead quality in his examples).
  • Monthly webinar with people client will work with if they hire you (delivery leaders, not only CEO face as ACV rises).
  • Thought leadership + lead gen in one motion.
  • Site/brand must feel enterprise if raising ACV — drop cheap “book a control call / explore partnership” energy.
  • LinkedIn webinar CAC often fails first-principles math vs Meta — webinars live on Meta.

2. LinkedIn

  • Historic home of gift-card customer generation at peak ~$270K/mo.
  • Still works evolved after commoditization.
  • Include content clips so not pure DR only.
  • List match via Primer; always test list vs native algo.
  • Peak proof: 10,000 accounts; 6,800 calls / 18 months; $6M→$22M.

Exact LinkedIn Message Ad (Directive Commerce) — Ad Library screenshot

He said this works like magic. Real Message Ad:

  • Advertiser: Directive (paid by directive consulting)
  • Format: Message Ad
  • Sender face: Lauren (Ritchie) Marks — division lead, not only the CEO
Hey %FIRSTNAME%, 29 mins, a $105 Amazon gift card, and you’ll find out if %COMPANYNAME%’s product feed is quietly killing your margins. I lead Directive Commerce, the only agency obsessed with B2B ecommerce advertising. A bad feed and poor ads don’t just lose clicks, it loses margin. We’ll show you exactly how to fix it. Take a call with one of our experts, and I’ll send you the gift card right after. Interested? [ Yes! Book Call ] [ Not sure? Tell me more ]

Gift is sent right after the call. Training match: ~7 years on this pattern; commerce → industrial TAM; charm pricing ($105 / 29 mins).

Copy rules baked into that ad

  • Charm timing: 29 mins not 30
  • Charm pricing: $105 not $100
  • Personalization: %FIRSTNAME% + %COMPANYNAME%
  • Short, scannable; problem → unique POV → CTA
  • Active CTA: Take a call / Book Call
  • Two buttons: hard yes + softer “tell me more”
  • Monetary incentive + commercial problem (feed/margins)

Meta webinar machine (real example + how the team runs it)

Webinar landing example (Directive)

  • Title: The Q4 Panic Playbook: 5 Tactics For Ecommerce Brands That Are Both B2B & B2C
  • Sub: When budgets tighten and timelines compress.
  • Format: 45-min virtual webinar
  • Scarcity: “Reserve Your Spot – Limited Availability”
  • Date example: September 16, 2026 · 12:30 PM EST
  • Form: First name + last name (simple)

Topic is timely + specific to a TAM (ecommerce brands straddling B2B&B2C), not generic “marketing tips.”

Meta/Facebook ads promoting that webinar (Ad Library style)

Multiple active sponsored creatives, same event, different angles — all video of real people (team faces), Sign up CTA:

  1. “Build Your Q4 Playbook” — Tighter budgets and shorter timelines demand smarter bets. Get a practical Q4 framework to capture demand, build buyer trust, and drive pipeline.
  2. “Q4 Is for B2B Too” — Q4 is not just for DTC. Learn how B2B ecommerce brands can capture high-value buyer demand with bolder campaigns that drive real pipeline.
  3. “Capture Q4 Demand” — Your high-value buyers are already in a Q4 shopping mindset. How bold creative and timely offers can turn seasonal demand into pipeline.

Pattern: one webinar, many ad angles; faces of practitioners on camera; “Live Webinar (Sep 16)” + Sign up.

How the team actually runs webinars (what you called out)

  1. AEs pitch the webinar — sales books / promotes attendance (the “get people in the door” motion).
  2. The team shows up and presents — it is not only the AE presenting solo.
  3. Delivery / specialists carry the content; AE owns pipeline follow-up from registrants and attendees.
  4. This matches his broader rule: upmarket brand is not only the CEO face — people you’d work with if you hired them are visible.
  5. Training context: Meta is where webinar paid works; sustained monthly execution (16–24 months) is what makes webinars “work,” not a one-off.

Brandastic webinar tickets

  1. One monthly (or bi-monthly) webinar on a locked vertical problem — specific title like their Q4 Panic Playbook, not vague.
  2. Landing: short form, limited seats, clear length (45 min).
  3. Meta ads: 3+ angles, real team faces on video, Sign up.
  4. AEs responsible for invites/pitch-to-register; experts present; AE owns post-webinar meetings.
  5. Do not make the AE the only person on stage.

4. Gift cards theory (why this beats free audits)

  • Search = intent, weak graphics for upmarket services story.
  • Social = graphics, weak intent → gift cards manufacture intent.
  • No gift unless they show up to the pitch.
  • Cost per held intro fell even including gift cost.
  • Activation > acquisition.
  • Method name: Customer Generation.

5. What never worked

  • “Unlock your audit” style non-incentive retarget — never worked for him (“dog shit”) despite good creative.
  • Do not make free-audit retarget the hero offer.

6. Ties to rest of system

  • Before: TAM + verify + Primer + positioning + Audience/Offer.
  • Alongside: HubSpot + Salesforce + Gong; proposal-held 20–40%.
  • After: CAPI; influence revenue (CTR ceiling ~4–6% → ~94% never click).
  • Creative: VP Marketing change agent, not SEO manager threat.

7. Brandastic tickets

  1. Webinars → Meta first; who is on camera.
  2. LinkedIn = ABM/content + optional customer-gen; don’t force webinar CAC.
  3. Incentives: use his ranked stack — AirPods → Kindle → ~$105 Amazon gift card; charm pricing ($105 / 29 min); max 3 lines; active CTA; gift only after show-up/call; track cost per held intro including gift COGS
  4. Kill free-audit retarget as primary.
  5. Primer list match; A/B list vs native.
  6. Report influence pipeline/revenue, not only CPL.
