How To Launch New Services — copyable playbook

This is not a summary. It is what they did, in order, with numbers and roles, so you can build Brandastic team plans from it.

The problem they solved

  • Tried to acquire a communications firm + ecommerce firm.
  • Founders they could afford < leaders already inside (Larissa, Lauren bar).
  • Weak contracts, weak depth, expensive multiples.
  • ~$45,000/month just for deal origination/management.
  • Switched to platform / incubation: build divisions internally.
  • Prior incubation proof: ~$3M P&L in <12 months.
  • Already ranking #1 in LLMs for “best B2B ecommerce agency / best B2B PR firm” before offering those services → launched them for real starting late Feb/March; website + kit live fast.

Org architecture they built (copy this structure)

  1. B2B TAM macros: every industry rolls into technology · industrial · services.
  2. Divisions: Performance (~$30M P&L) · Commerce (new) · Communications (new, reverse-integrating “Abe”/earned media into performance).
  3. CS division: any client >$20K with multiple services gets an Account Director (end siloed SEO/media/creative each running separate weekly meetings → churn).
  4. Account Director pay: ~$151–175K base, OTE under $200K; moved strong strategists into AD roles at same salary to avoid turnover before they were “director ready.”
  5. Big-account pairing: $20K+ or $50K+ deals → Account Director paired with AE on the pitch because buyers ask “who’s on my account?”

Exact launch kit (communications example + same pattern for commerce)

They ran Mon+Thu 1-hour meetings with: head of comms, commerce, sales, marketing, CS, finance, HR. CEO gave blank roadmaps (status template) then leaders filled.

  1. Hire depth first: Comms leader (Larissa) got one hire — Head of PR from NP Digital (performance-shop background + LLM/PR POV). Always want two people in a new competency.
  2. Paid social under comms: ~15-person dept, ~$3M P&L already existed — easier sell first (“$15K/mo, rank better on LLMs / digital PR”) before wild brand stunts.
  3. Market research (commerce): head-of-marketing interviews (Grainger, Snap-on, Schneider-class). Questions in the weeds: agency problems, Feedonomics, what they’d do different. No methodology until research done. Same questions + recorder.
  4. POV → methodology decks: Discoverability OS split in two — non-ecommerce vs B2B ecommerce (contribution margin differs). Comms integrated into performance as earned+owned POV.
  5. Decks: overall B2B comms deck + paid social + influencer + PR decks (paid social already existed).
  6. SOWs for each offer.
  7. North Star Metric (NSM) tracker — honorable to show if paid work works.
  8. Website: service pages, methodology pages, proposal pages, sub-brand guidelines, logo for division.
  9. Content pack: listicle(s) + guide(s) + ~5 thought-leadership pieces (or two guides).
  10. PR tech choice: Meltwater over Precision (their note).
  11. Bring tech/content from existing stack (“AEB”).
  12. People trigger: middle manager / AE only after ~five strategists or five accounts. Commerce lead waited longer without paid-social revenue. “Protect them from expectations” — extra resource = honest trade of faster goal timeline (e.g. 3 months faster).
  13. ~30 days to stand up a new department when kit exists.
  14. Salesforce routing: parent/child so form fills don’t break SF; new division object/field; children under performance/commerce/comms; keep normal product names; report pipe by division.
  15. Legal: contracts must be assignable to any business entity (helps multi-brand and future asset sale).
  16. Pricing engine: post-intro debrief → hours by service at cost rate × regional multiplier × fully weighted overhead → price by division/capability.
  17. Intro call redesign: meet them where they are, expand to the outcome not only the service they named.
  18. GTM dependencies checklist they listed: PR motion, budget allocation by channel/TAM/offer, creative briefs, videos, brand campaigns, organic content for rank, messaging to return POCs about new offers, client-strategy reporting, rate cards, SLAs, bonus plans, KPIs, banking, HR/columns, state taxes, etc.

ICP / personas (critical)

  • Primary personas: VP of Marketing and Director of Marketing.
  • They do not have easy access to CRO / VP Sales — so they refuse services outside marketing leaders’ KPIs (e.g. don’t pretend sales-ops is a primary new-biz motion into sales leaders).
  • Stay inside archetype KPIs while expanding services → that’s how the engine scales.
  • RFP rule: only offer a service if you could win the best account’s RFP in that category. Cross-sell-only services forbidden (video example: lost every real video RFP).
  • No-brainer: SEO shop adds paid media around $1.5–3M revenue.
  • MVD: revops may stay small P&L but required to connect CRM/Salesforce for big spend.

NRR / comp (so launch doesn’t die to churn)

  • Path past ~$20M in their category (Power, Tenility, PMG pattern): net revenue retention.
  • Every middle manager / leader / strategist: comp + bonus for offsetting churn via upsell, cross-sell, referral, or return POC.
  • 100% NRR month → bonus, divided by 12, accrues monthly.
  • Warning: service addressable market (SAM) has diminishing returns — can’t infinitely attach 5 services into the same 100 accounts → new divisions still needed, carefully.
  • TAM almost never truly saturated; SAM is the real constraint.

Three first principles they said must be true

  1. Can win new business into the new division (not only cross-sell).
  2. Intro calls expand prospect to the outcome.
  3. Services integrate clearly in methodology pitch + client strategy delivery.
