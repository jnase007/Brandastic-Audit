How To Launch New Services/Offerings — full playbook

This page is the long document. Scroll it all. Print it. Turn every numbered block into team tickets. Not a skim.

Purpose: copy what they did. Tools, numbers, personas, artifacts, comps, CRM, legal, research questions — all below.

1. Why they stopped buying agencies

  • Tried acquiring a communications firm and an ecommerce firm.
  • Founders they could afford were not better than leaders already inside (bar named: Larissa, Lauren).
  • Targets had revenue but weak contracts, weak differentiation, weak strategic depth.
  • Paying a multiple on revenue did not buy enough other assets (team quality, IP, process).
  • Work-ethic / expectation mismatch with sellers (part of why they were for sale).
  • Just running deal origination + management cost about $45,000/month.
  • Message to sub-$10M agencies: this path is often a trap.
  • Switched to platform / co-strategy and internal incubation of divisions.
  • Prior proof incubation worked: about $3M P&L in under 12 months.
  • Already ranking #1 in LLMs for “best B2B ecommerce agency / best B2B PR firm” before offering those services — signal to launch for real (late Feb/March start; website + kit live quickly).

2. Org architecture to copy

B2B industry macros

Every B2B industry rolls into exactly three buckets:

  1. Technology
  2. Industrial
  3. Services

Divisions

  • Performance — core, about $30M P&L
  • Commerce — new division
  • Communications — new division; reverse-integrate earned media (“Abe”) into performance POV as earned + owned

CS / Account Director layer

  • Trigger: client spend > $20K AND multiple services
  • Gets an Account Director
  • Why: SEO strategist + media strategist + creative strategist each running separate weekly client meetings → inefficiency + churn
  • Centralize multi-service >$20K under one AD

Account Director pay (their numbers)

  • Base about $151K–$175K
  • OTE under $200K
  • Moved strong strategists into AD roles at same salary to reduce turnover before they were “director ready,” while giving a path

Big-deal sales staffing

  • For offers about $20K+ or $50K+: pair Account Director + AE on the pitch
  • Buyer’s real question: “Who is on my account?”

Launch operating meetings

  • Monday + Thursday, 1 hour
  • Required: head of comms, commerce, sales, marketing, CS, finance, HR
  • CEO gives roadmap templates (status columns) blank; leaders fill dates/notes
  • CEO designs structure; leaders own the how

3. ICP / personas (copy exactly into your ICP doc)

Primary personas they sell to

  1. VP of Marketing
  2. Director of Marketing

Who they do not easily reach

  • CRO / VP of Sales — weak relationships, hard new points of contact

Service design consequence

  • Do not offer services outside marketing-leader KPIs as primary new-biz motions
  • Example failure mode: sales-ops as a lead motion into sales leaders when you only know marketing buyers
  • Expand services inside archetype KPIs — that is how expansion stays coherent

RFP rule (non-negotiable)

  • Only offer a service if you could win the best account’s RFP in that category
  • If not → do not offer it
  • Historical miss: offered video; got destroyed on real video RFPs
  • Never offer cross-sell-only services — you run out of accounts to attach to; no lasting GP; agency becomes unfocused

No-brainer path

  • SEO shop → add paid media around $1.5M–$3M revenue
  • Refuse ad-hoc “client wants email and we’re not experts”

MVD — Minimum Viable Department

  • Some capabilities never become huge P&Ls but are required
  • Example: RevOps — may stall around smaller P&L (~$3M thinking) but required to connect CRM/Salesforce for serious spend
  • Video: keep as creative inside paid (MVD), not fake standalone video agency

4. Exact launch kit — artifacts to create (ticket list)

Used for Communications and Commerce. Same pattern for any new division/service.

A. People

  1. Hire craft depth first (example: Head of PR from NP Digital — performance-shop background + strongest LLM/PR POV)
  2. Always try for two people in a new competency
  3. Existing leader may have adjacent experience but not full depth — one expert hire under them
  4. Paid social under comms already existed (~15 people, ~$3M P&L) — easier first wedge: “~$15K/mo, rank better on LLMs / digital PR” before expensive brand stunts
  5. Do not give middle manager / AE on day one
  6. Trigger example: after ~five strategists or ~five accounts → manager training + onboarding
  7. Commerce lead waited longer without paid-social revenue
  8. Philosophy: best way to love someone is protect them from expectations
  9. Resource-ask script: “I hear you. Are you willing to hit the goal 3 months faster? That is the exchange.”

B. Market research (when you are weak)

  1. Interview heads of marketing (examples named: Grainger, Snap-on, Schneider-class industrial)
  2. Weeds questions: agency problems, tools like Feedonomics, what they would do different, why
  3. Same question set every interview + recorder
  4. Do not invent methodology until research is finished
  5. CEO does not micromanage booking method — requires research outcome

C. Methodology and sales assets

  1. Build POV from research
  2. Discoverability OS decks — two parallel versions:
    • Non-ecommerce clients
    • B2B ecommerce clients (contribution margin / ecommerce reality differs; one deck creates sales friction)
  3. Integrate communications into performance methodology as earned + owned media POV
  4. Decks to build:
    • Overall B2B communications deck
    • Paid social deck (already existed for them)
    • Influencer deck
    • PR deck
  5. SOWs for each offer
  6. North Star Metric (NSM) tracker — if you take money, show whether it works
  7. Proposal pages

D. Website + brand

  1. Service pages
  2. Methodology pages
  3. Proposal pages
  4. Division logo
  5. Sub-brand guidelines for the division
  6. Content pack: listicle(s) + guide(s) + ~5 thought-leadership pieces (or two guides) — same family as Video 01’s 8-piece system
  7. Bring technology/content from existing stack
  8. PR tech choice note: Meltwater over Precision

E. Systems

  1. Salesforce parent/child so multi-select form fills do not break SF
    • New object/field for division
    • Children under performance / commerce / communications
    • Keep normal product name conventions
    • Report pipeline by division
  2. Legal: contracts assignable to any of your businesses (multi-brand + future asset sale)
  3. Pricing model: post-intro debrief → hours by service × cost rate × regional multiplier × fully weighted overhead → price by division/capability
  4. Intro call redesign: meet them where they are; expand to the outcome, not only the named service; open adjacent offers that serve that outcome

F. Timing

  • New department can stand up in about ~30 days when kit exists
  • CEO = what/why macros; leaders fill how so they emotionally own it for 12 months

G. Full GTM dependency list they called out (for roadmaps)

  • PR motion
  • Budget allocation by channel × TAM × size × offer
  • Creative briefs, videos, brand campaigns
  • Organic content required to rank
  • Messaging to return points of contact about new offerings
  • Client strategy reporting
  • Titles, relationship capacities, JDs, comp plans, hiring meetings
  • Sales process involvement; white space mapping
  • Finance: bonus plans, legal, service-level budgets, division budgets, KPIs, banking
  • HR: rate cards/columns, values, state taxes, transitions
  • Training, onboarding, SOPs documented as you go; hire experts who bring SOPs when entering a new craft

5. Three first principles that must be true

  1. Can win new business into the new division (not only cross-sell)
  2. Intro calls expand the prospect to the outcome
  3. Services integrate clearly in methodology pitch + client strategy delivery

6. NRR / comp engine (so launches survive churn)

  • Hardest honest agency problem: sustaining success
  • Path past ~$20M in their peer set (Power / Tenility / PMG pattern): net revenue retention
  • Every middle manager / leader / strategist: base + bonus for offsetting churn via upsell, cross-sell, referral, or return POC
  • 100% NRR month → bonus ÷ 12, accrues monthly
  • Cannot outrun churn with new biz alone at scale
  • Warning: service addressable market (SAM) has diminishing returns — cannot infinitely attach 5 services into the same 100 accounts
  • TAM almost never truly saturated; SAM is the real constraint → careful new divisions still needed

7. Brandastic ticket list (start here)

  1. Write ICP one-pager: VP/Director Marketing (or proven buyers), firmographics, exclusions
  2. Service audit sheet: Service | Win best RFP? | Cross-sell only? | Keep/MVD/Kill | Owner
  3. Pick 0–1 incubation after vertical lock (Video 01)
  4. If 1: clone Section 4 kit into Asana/Notion with owners + dates
  5. CRM division field + reporting
  6. Contract assignability review with counsel
  7. NRR behaviors for AMs (even before fancy bonus math)
  8. Default: build, do not buy an agency unless operator clears leadership bar
