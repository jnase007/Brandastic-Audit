First principles: TAM, positioning, Primer, verify, Audience + Offer

This is the foundation before creative or channel spend. Full detail from the advertising training so you can build a TAM SOP and offer rules for Brandastic.

His spine: Positioning → real account TAM → verify hard → Audience + Offer → then ads. Creative is downstream. Tools named: Primer, AI list-check agents, Meta/LinkedIn list vs native tests.

1. Positioning first (triple niche)

  • If the agency “boils the ocean,” ads cannot also boil the ocean — they will not work.
  • He calls good positioning a triple niche: something for someone in some industry.
  • Directive example he used: Demand Generation for Directors of Demand Gen in B2B SaaS (something · someone · industry).
  • Positioning must be tight enough that a paid audience list can be defined and verified.
  • Website/org can show more than you advertise — but paid must stay focused (see also division + TAM spend rules on Channels / Brand structure).

2. Build a real TAM (not outbound junk)

  • First principle of advertising your agency: build your total addressable market.
  • This is not the shitty list outbound agencies use.
  • Emails are free to send. Ads are not free. Only put paid media against accounts you actually want.
  • It is an account-level list (companies you want to break into), not a random email dump.
  • List scale he described in training/ops: start around ~20,000, curate toward ~10,000 ideal accounts — a year-class curation job tied to ICP, not a weekend export. Peak scale context: he ran ~10,000 target accounts → 6,800 sales calls in 18 months → $6M → $22M with heavy LinkedIn (~$270K/mo at peak).

3. How they build and verify the list (exact process)

  1. Start wide — example: start around 20,000 accounts.
  2. AI first pass — AI agents click websites / filter to shrink the list (example: down to ~8,000).
  3. Human/team verify the remaining accounts (this is real work; he has a team do verification — he does not personally verify every row day-to-day).
  4. CEO “100 random scrolls” QA (his exact method):
    • Scroll the list and stop 100 times at random.
    • If ~90 of 100 stops are legit accounts you actually want, the list is good enough.
    • There will always be some misses/disagreements — 90/100 is the bar before spending your own money.
  5. Time reality: whatever you think verification takes, you are about 10× under. It can take a week. Do it.
  6. Story: ads not working with same copy/offer → he paused all ads, forced list re-check; list went 24,000 → 16,000 after cleanup. List quality was the problem.
  7. Mindset: this is your money (bonus/distribution you are not taking) — do not spend it on a dirty list.

4. Primer + list vs native

  • He uses Primer to upload the verified TAM into ad platforms.
  • Modern Meta algorithms can find audiences without first-party lists — he still tests both every time.
  • Always A/B:
    • List-based / matched (Primer upload of your TAM)
    • Native algorithm audiences
  • Why keep the list: control — the accounts you actually care about, not only what the algo optimizes for short-term.

5. Audience + Offer (the whole game)

  • He repeats it: advertising comes down to Audience and Offer every time.
  • Creative is downstream of those two.
  • Scaled offer system: Customer Generation — gift-card / free-product incentives for show-up (see Channels for full stack).
  • His ranked free products:
    1. AirPods
    2. Kindle
    3. Amazon gift card ~$105 (charm pricing; 29 minutes not 30)
  • Non-monetary lead magnets: he has not found one that works well (“unlock your audit” fails).
  • Full magic LinkedIn ad copy + webinar machine: Channels page.

6. Who the audience is (ties to ICP)

  • Advertise to change-agent buyers (e.g. VP of Marketing), not threatened practitioners (not SEO manager for SEO ads).
  • Advertise division/department + outcome, not capability laundry lists.
  • Only spend against TAM slices with CAC room (for them: technology + industrial; skip low-spend services TAM for paid).
  • Know which audience lives where (e.g. industrial/manufacturing marketers heavier on Meta; SaaS-y marketers heavier on LinkedIn).

7. Brandastic TAM SOP (tickets)

  1. Write triple-niche positioning one-pager (something · someone · industry) for Brandastic.
  2. Build account-level TAM spreadsheet for locked verticals only.
  3. AI pass + human verify; Justin does 100 random scrolls before spend.
  4. Bar: ~90/100 legit before turning ads on.
  5. Load via Primer (or equivalent); always test list vs native.
  6. Define Offer from ranked stack (AirPods / Kindle / $105 Amazon) before creative scale.
  7. Do not run paid on a list you would not bet your own distribution on.
