03 · Google SEO

SEO findings: moat is unfinished

City property-management pages exist (Dallas, San Diego, Nashville, Chicago, Atlanta…). That’s the right architecture. Rankings and internal priority still favor brand and mixed-audience paths.

1
Organic is brand-heavy
Top traffic terms: “rhome”, “rhome property management”, official-website variants. Fine for retention — weak for new landlord acquisition in-market.
2
High-intent landlord queries are open
Examples (US vol / CPC): property management san diego 1,900 / $5.26 · company near me 2,900 / $5.24 · dallas 880 / $5.43 · nashville 590 / $5.57 · hire a property manager 590 / $7.58. These should map to owner LPs + local pages.
3
Dual-audience homepage dilutes SEO + paid
Title/desc lean investors; H1 is generic (“Find Everything You Need”). Owners, Residents, For Rent, Acquisitions share one roof — Google and users get mixed intent.
4
Subdomains / city variants split equity
Semrush hits saltlake.rhomepm.com, chicago.rhomepm.com, nashville.rhomepm.com alongside www city URLs. Consolidate signals; one canonical pattern per market.
5
Renter/content noise in organic mix
Terms like short-term lease, rent stabilized, resident benefits package pull non-landlord journeys. Keep pages — don’t let them own the growth narrative in reporting.
6
Trust / CTA hygiene
Header social icons → # (dead). Contact often via Locations. Owner form path needs to be the hero on owner-intent pages for SEO and ads message match.
